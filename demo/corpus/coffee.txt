Coffee is a brewed drink prepared from the roasted and ground seeds of certain berries, which are commonly called coffee beans. The beans come from shrubs of the genus Coffea, and the two species grown on the largest scale are Coffea arabica, usually known simply as Arabica, and Coffea canephora, widely called Robusta. Coffee contains caffeine, a natural stimulant that increases alertness and reduces the feeling of tiredness, which is a large part of the drink's appeal. According to tradition the plant was first recognised in the highlands of Ethiopia, and the practice of brewing coffee later spread through the Arabian Peninsula and the wider Islamic world before reaching Europe. To make the drink, ripe berries are harvested, the seeds are separated and dried, and the resulting green beans are roasted at high temperature, which develops their characteristic flavour and aroma. The roasted beans are then ground and combined with hot water using one of many methods, such as filter or drip brewing, the pressurised technique that produces espresso, or steeping in a press. Coffee is one of the most heavily traded commodities in the world and forms an important part of the economies of many countries that grow it near the equator. It is also woven into social life, and cafes have long served as places for people to meet and talk. Different growing regions and roasting styles give coffees a wide range of tastes. The two main species differ in taste and hardiness, with Arabica prized for its smoother flavour and Robusta valued for its strength and higher caffeine content. After the drink reached Europe, coffee houses became lively centres of conversation, business, and the exchange of news.
