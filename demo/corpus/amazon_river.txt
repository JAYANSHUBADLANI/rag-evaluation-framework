The Amazon is a great river of South America and the largest river in the world measured by the volume of water it discharges into the sea. It carries more water than the next several largest rivers combined, and at its mouth it pours an enormous flow into the Atlantic Ocean. Estimates of its length are close to 6,400 kilometres, which places it in close rivalry with the Nile for the title of the longest river on Earth. The river rises in the Andes Mountains of Peru and flows generally eastward across the continent before reaching the Atlantic on the coast of Brazil. Along the way it gathers water from more than a thousand tributaries, several of which are themselves among the largest rivers in the world. The Amazon drains a vast basin covering much of northern South America, a region blanketed by the largest tropical rainforest on the planet. This rainforest is home to a remarkable share of the world's known species of plants, insects, birds, and mammals. During the wet season the river floods large areas of surrounding forest, creating flooded woodlands that many fish exploit for food. The river has no bridges across its main course for most of its length, and boats remain the principal means of travel for the communities that live along its banks. For a long time the exact source of the Amazon was disputed, with several Andean streams competing for the title. The river grows so wide in places during the flood season that a person standing on one bank cannot see the other.
