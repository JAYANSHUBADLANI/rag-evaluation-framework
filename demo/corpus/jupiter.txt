Jupiter is the largest planet in the Solar System and the fifth in order of distance from the Sun. It is a gas giant, composed mainly of hydrogen and helium, without a solid surface like that of the rocky inner planets. Jupiter is so massive that its mass is more than twice that of all the other planets combined, yet it spins faster than any other planet, completing a rotation in under ten hours. Its atmosphere is marked by colourful bands of clouds and by enormous storms, the most famous of which is the Great Red Spot, a swirling tempest larger than the Earth that has persisted for centuries. The planet is orbited by a large family of moons, of which the four largest were discovered by Galileo Galilei in 1610 and are therefore called the Galilean moons. These four, named Io, Europa, Ganymede, and Callisto, are worlds in their own right; Ganymede is the largest moon in the Solar System, and Europa is thought to hide a salty ocean beneath its icy crust. Jupiter also possesses a faint ring system and a powerful magnetic field far stronger than Earth's. Because of its great mass, the planet influences the orbits of many small bodies and is sometimes described as helping to shield the inner Solar System from comets. Several spacecraft have visited Jupiter to study its atmosphere, moons, and magnetic environment. A broad band of much smaller bodies, the asteroid belt, lies between Jupiter and Mars, and Jupiter's strong gravity shapes the orbits within it. The planet radiates more heat than it receives from the Sun, a sign that it is still slowly contracting and cooling.
