William Shakespeare was an English playwright, poet, and actor who is widely regarded as the greatest writer in the English language and the world's most celebrated dramatist. He was born in the town of Stratford-upon-Avon in 1564 and later moved to London, where he worked in the theatre both as a writer and as a performer. Over his career he produced about thirty-seven plays and a sequence of one hundred and fifty-four sonnets, along with several longer poems. His plays are usually grouped into comedies, histories, and tragedies, and among the best known are the tragedies Hamlet, Macbeth, Othello, and Romeo and Juliet. Shakespeare was a part-owner of the playing company that performed many of his works at the Globe Theatre, an open-air playhouse on the south bank of the River Thames in London. His writing is admired for its rich characters, its insight into human nature, and its inventive use of language, and he coined or popularised many words and phrases still used today. The plays were first collected and printed together in 1623, seven years after his death, in a volume now known as the First Folio. Shakespeare died in 1616 in the town where he had been born. His works have been translated into every major language and are performed more often than those of any other playwright. Centuries after his death, his influence on literature and theatre remains profound. Little is known for certain about several periods of his life, a gap that has fed centuries of speculation about the man behind the plays. Many everyday expressions in English, from breaking the ice to a wild-goose chase, can be traced back to his writing.
